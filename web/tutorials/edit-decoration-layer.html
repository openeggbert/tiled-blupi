Tutorial 5: Edit the Decoration Layer

The Decoration layer holds purely visual tiles — trees, flowers, signs, atmospheric scenery — that are displayed on top of the terrain but do not affect gameplay. This tutorial explains how to work with this layer and highlights the important difference between the Decoration and Foreground layers.

Tileset used: The Decoration layer uses the explo tileset, which has a special y-offset of 64 pixels (tiles appear lower than their grid position). See the explo tileset page for details.

Step 1: Select the Decoration Layer

  1. In the Layers panel, click the row labeled Decoration.
  2. Confirm in the status bar that "Decoration" is the active layer.
Do not confuse Decoration with Foreground. Tiles on the Decoration layer have no collision — the player and enemies pass through them. Placing a wall tile on the Decoration layer will render it visually but the player will walk through it.

Step 2: Select the explo Tileset

  1. In the Tilesets panel, select the explo tileset tab.
  2. Browse the available decoration tiles.

Step 3: Understanding the y=64 Offset

The explo tileset has a tileoffset of y=64 pixels defined in its .tsx file. This means every tile from this tileset is rendered 64 pixels below its nominal grid position when displayed in the game and in Tiled.

Practical consequence: if you want a decoration tile to appear visually aligned with row 5 on the canvas, you must place it at row 3 in Tiled (since 2 rows × 32 pixels/row = 64 pixels of downward shift). The exact adjustment depends on the tile's visual size.

Why does this offset exist? It is a design choice in the original game's rendering engine. The explo tileset tiles are meant to sit "on top of" terrain cells, and the 64px shift achieves this visual alignment. tiled-blupi reproduces this offset in the TSX definition so Tiled's preview matches the game.

Step 4: Paint Decoration Tiles

  1. Press B for the Stamp Brush.
  2. Click a decoration tile in the explo tileset panel.
  3. Click on the canvas to paint the tile at the desired position.

The same brush, eraser, and fill tools from Tutorial 4 apply here.

Step 5: Erase Decoration Tiles

  1. Make sure the Decoration layer is active.
  2. Press E for the Eraser.
  3. Click or drag over the decoration tiles you want to remove.

Erasing on the Decoration layer removes only decoration tiles; the Foreground tiles beneath are unaffected.

Step 6: View Both Layers Together

To see how the Decoration layer looks on top of the terrain:

  • Make sure both the Foreground and Decoration layers have their eye icon visible (click the eye to toggle visibility).
  • Toggle the Decoration layer's eye off to see just the terrain; toggle it back on to see decorations overlaid.
